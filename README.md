# Nocturnal Internet Use and its Association with Behavioral, Psychological, and Sociodemographic Variables Among University Students in Bangladesh

## 📌 Project Overview
This repository contains the documentation, research framework, and dataset analysis for the published academic study titled **"Nocturnal Internet Use and its Association with Behavioral, Psychological, and Sociodemographic Variables among University Students in Bangladesh"** (Published in *Discovery Education*, Springer Nature, 2026)[cite: 1, 2]. 

The study investigates late-night screen time, psychological factors, and lifestyle variables based on a cross-sectional survey conducted among university students[cite: 3].

---

## 📊 Methodology & Study Design
* **Study Design:** A cross-sectional survey conducted among undergraduate students at Rajshahi University, Bangladesh[cite: 3, 7].
* **Sample Size:** 361 valid participants (59.3% male, 40.7% female; 42.9% 3rd-year and 57.1% 4th-year students)[cite: 3, 10].
* **Data Collection & Tools:** Data was gathered using structured questionnaires during regular classroom sessions[cite: 7, 8]. Standardized tools included Young's Internet Addiction Test (IAT) and the Kessler Psychological Distress Scale (K-6)[cite: 9].
* **Statistical Analysis:** Utilized SPSS 22.0 for descriptive statistics, chi-square tests, and binary logistic regression analysis[cite: 9, 10].

---

## 💡 Key Findings & Insights
* **High Prevalence of NIU:** The overall prevalence of Nocturnal Internet Use (NIU)—defined as using the internet for >2 hours at night and >4 days a week—was found to be **71.2%** among university students[cite: 3, 9, 11].
* **Major Associated Factors (Binary Logistic Regression):**
  * **Academic Year:** 4th-year senior students showed significantly higher odds of NIU compared to 3rd-year students (AOR = 2.21)[cite: 3, 16].
  * **Marital Status:** Unmarried students exhibited a higher likelihood of late-night internet engagement than married peers (AOR = 2.80)[cite: 3, 16].
  * **Parental Education:** Students whose fathers held a graduate-level education showed increased odds of NIU (AOR = 3.58)[cite: 3, 17].
  * **Behavioral Purpose & Addiction:** Using the internet without study purposes (AOR = 3.24) and meeting the criteria for internet addiction (AOR = 3.75) were strongly and significantly predictive of nocturnal internet habits[cite: 3, 17].
